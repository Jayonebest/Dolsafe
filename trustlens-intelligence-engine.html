⚠️ MAC randomization — why TrustLens does not rely on MAC alone
Modern iPhones (iOS 14+), Android devices (Android 10+), and Windows 10+ randomize their MAC address per network, per session, and periodically over time. A device that was A4:C3:F0:72:8B:1D this morning may present as B7:2E:44:9A:1C:F8 this afternoon. Relying on MAC alone would produce a new "first-time visitor" every hour from the same device.
TrustLens response: multi-signal device continuity
📡 RF signal fingerprint
Probe request timing patterns, signal strength profile, channel preference, and burst frequency create a device behavioral fingerprint even when MAC rotates
📷 Camera movement track
Body shape, gait signature, clothing color pattern, and movement trajectory link camera observations to RF signals without facial recognition
📍 Space-time correlation
If a body appears at zone A at T=0 and a RF signal appears at zone A at T=0, they are likely the same person regardless of MAC rotation

⚠️ Failure modes and safeguards
False positive (civilian flagged as threat)
Firearm detected → LE check fails → alert sent to human dispatcher → dispatcher makes final call. TrustLens never dispatches armed response automatically for weapon detection. The auto-dispatch (98% confidence) applies to behavioral threat convergence — weapon detection always routes to human review first.
False negative (threat mistaken for LE)
If an attacker acquires LE uniform and radio: behavioral analysis still runs. Pre-attack stress signature (HR, gait, thermal) would be inconsistent with trained officer profile. Zone behavior (recon, loitering) would remain flagged. Multi-signal fusion makes pure visual spoofing insufficient.

Hashed MAC as personal data — the legal nuance
A hashed MAC address can theoretically constitute personal data under GDPR if it can be used to single out an individual over time. TrustLens addresses this with three architectural defenses:
⏱️
Mandatory expiry (TTL)
Data that expires after 72 hours cannot be used to build a longitudinal profile. After expiry, the same device is a first-time visitor. No continuity across TTL boundaries = no meaningful personal data under GDPR Art.5(e).
🔀
MAC randomization compound
Modern devices rotate their MAC address. A hash of a randomized MAC is pseudorandom — it changes every session. TrustLens hashes what the device presents, which is already anonymized by the device itself.
🏛️
Public safety legal basis
GDPR Art.6(1)(e) permits processing necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest. Transit security is explicitly a public interest task. The Surveillance Impact Assessment (Extended tier) documents this legal basis formally.
